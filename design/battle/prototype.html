Battle plan, redesigned

A prototype of the battle prompt built from design/design-language.html. It uses the same game as the screenshots: Guild defending Habbanya Sietch against Atreides, Advanced Combat on, 6 forces there and 13 spice. The controls work, so try the steppers, leaders and cards. The buttons above the card switch between a few situations, including a second game with special forces.

Today, with the spacing fixes

What changes, and why

  1. The battle is the act card. Parchment with the gold rule, as the design language's "one thing to do" principle asks. There's no title. The kicker names the territory, and one line compares both sides' forces, special forces and who is aggressor.
  2. Strength is the headline. Your total is dial plus leader (Battle.DetermineBattleOutcome). It updates as you change things.
  3. Dial and spice are the wheel; the tokens are the forces. The rulebook's battle plan is a strength and the spice to pay for it (page 13, Advanced Combat), and the tokens show which forces that commits: solid is full strength, half-filled is half, hollow stays out. Clicking a token cycles only that token (out, half, full, out), and the dial and spice follow. Turning a dial changes as few tokens as it can: dial up ½ turns one hollow token half; spice up 1 at the same dial turns two half tokens into one full and one hollow. A step that no mix can reach is disabled.
  4. Special forces get their own row, above the other forces, at the same size. A star badge on the edge marks them, the option picked in design/special-forces.html. Try the Special forces state, which uses the rulebook's example: 1 Sardaukar and 5 forces, dial 3, spice 1. The rulebook lets the winner pick which forces make up the dial when taking losses. Here that's 1 full and 4 half forces, or 1 Sardaukar and 2 half forces. The app fixes the mix in the plan (Game_Battle.cs:742). The starting plan keeps your special forces; click a token to change that. The mix also decides which undialled forces a Diplomat can retreat and how Graduate and Ixian loss substitution play out. Picking it after winning, as the rulebook has it, would mean a change to the game engine.engine change
  5. Smaller leader discs. The portraits already carry name and strength, and all five leaders fit on one row. Once you pick one, the others grey out; the same goes for weapon and defense, where None counts as a pick. The design language asks for a gold halo instead, so that selected and unavailable stop looking alike; greyed-out means unavailable leaders will need a different mark.
  6. Weapon and defense are always there, with "None" as a real option, so the form keeps the same shape whatever you hold.
  7. The button says why it's off, as the design language's button rules ask.

Not shown: ally forces and ally spice, Kwisatz Haderach, the Banker bonus and Voice. Ally forces would be another token group in the matchup line.
