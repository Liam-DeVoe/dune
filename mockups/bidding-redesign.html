A tighter bidding card

Today the bidding prompt is two parchment boxes: the shared status header, then the bidding panel. The proposal folds them into one card that's about a quarter shorter (310px against 415px). It also states the facts players currently piece together on their own: the current bid, your spice, who's still in, and what the card is.

Today and proposed

  1. One card, not two. The status header's prompt line ("Please bid or pass.") and its sigil row move into the bidding card. The sigil row becomes the order line, and the header goes away for this phase. The design language already asks for this: the form lives inside the prompt, not in a second panel.
  2. The facts are a sentence. "Fremen bid 1. You have 10." Today the bid is a chip and a number with no verb, and your spice is only in the header above.
  3. The order line is labelled. Today it's six sigils on card backs: the faction's hand size, with the current bidder drawn slightly bigger. Now each entry is named and shows, in turn order, who is up (gold), who holds the bid (with the amount), who passed (struck out), and hand size. A full hand shows in red, as it does today.
  4. One gold button. Bid is gold. Pass is outlined and pushed to the far right, so a quick click doesn't hit the wrong one. Today both are gold and sit side by side.
  5. The card is named. When you can see it, through prescience or a faction-sold card, the text says what it is. At 52px the art alone can't be read. When you can't see it, the card back is dropped, because it tells you nothing.
  6. Quick values. "+1", "+2", and "max 10" under the field, per the design language's "type it, don't tap it". Getting to 8 from 2 is one click instead of six.
  7. Auto-pass says what it does. The threshold sits on the same line as the checkbox, so the setting reads as one sentence. It's also there while you wait, which is when you'd set it.
  8. Timer only when there is one. "1184:49:37" is the elapsed time on a game with no limit. Show a countdown only when the host set one.

Other states

The same parts cover every case the current panel handles: ally and Emperor contributions, Karama, and silent auctions. Nothing moves between states, so the eye stays in one place.
